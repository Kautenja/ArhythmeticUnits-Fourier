\section{Complete-Pipeline Prototype Evaluation}
\label{sec:pipeline-evaluation}
A second campaign informed the production choice. Its exact sources and raw
summaries are archived separately in \path{docs/whitepaper/data/pipeline/}. The
prototypes compare: (i) the original arithmetic with controlled exact cadence
(\code{legacy}); (ii) candidate arithmetic executed in boundary bursts across four stage slots
(\code{stage\_burst}); (iii) a serial balanced sequence within one hop
(\code{serial}); and (iv) four overlapped stages across four hops
(\code{pipeline}). Modes (ii)--(iv) share positive-bin specialization and
prepared smoothing bounds. The stage-burst and overlapped modes share storage and four-hop latency and
therefore isolate placement most closely. Serial also changes stage storage
and latency; its comparison with stage bursts is informative but cannot
isolate dispatch cost alone.

The host and compiler are the Apple M1 Pro and Apple Clang 21.0.0 described
above. All modes use C++11 with \code{-O3 -DNDEBUG -Wall -Wextra -pedantic},
scalar float, deterministic noise, normalized periodic Hann, $\alpha=0.8$,
and 48 kHz. Sizes are $N\in\{1024,4096,16384\}$, hops $H=N/4,N/2$, and
smoothing off or one-third octave. Twelve repetitions rotate the mode order;
each pass has 20 warmup and 64 measured hops. Separate passes measure total
hop time, individual samples, and synchronous 16-, 64-, and 256-sample blocks.
These are simulated blocks, not device callbacks. Empty timer median/p99 is
41/42 ns, with no subtraction. Historical phase timings separately confirm
that packing, reconstruction, and postprocessing remain concentrated.

Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} shows an approximately 80\% reduction in the median
observed block maximum for serial versus legacy in this workload. Serial's
51.392 microseconds per hop exceeds the stage-burst control's 50.327. The
fully matched overlap comparison also costs more, at 55.145 microseconds:
scheduling is not free. Across all 12 size/hop/smoothing settings, median paired
serial/legacy total-time ratios span 0.920--1.212; overlap/legacy spans
0.986--1.585. Peak reduction can coexist with greater total CPU cost. Four-hop
overlap adds $3H$ samples of age: 64 ms here and 256 ms for $N=16384,H=4096$.
It did not earn that extra latency and storage in this campaign.

The measured prototypes are not the integrated production implementation.
Their arithmetic, cache policy, and adapters informed the successor, which
subsequently gained maximum-size plans, live cache rebuilding, SIMD support,
and module-owned output. Do not use these timings as a production speedup.
The campaign retains all per-repetition summaries, including preemption-sensitive
maxima, but no individual call traces. It remains a single-host study without
Rack graphics, actual audio callbacks, or an optimized-library baseline.

Production correctness is checked separately: 1,934,764 standalone assertions
in seven cases cover spectra, cadence, retained-input wraparound, all 15 window
functions, cache invalidation, reset, and allocation-free live changes. A
separate Rack-linked suite includes scalar/SIMD agreement and coordinate
mapping (3,807,481 assertions in four cases); module lifecycle and serialization
suites also pass. The standalone suite passes AddressSanitizer and
UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer. A Rack plugin build and native display inspection
with context recreation pass, but no full interactive Rack/DAW workload or
integrated production timing campaign was performed. These checks support the
implementation contract, not a device deadline guarantee.
