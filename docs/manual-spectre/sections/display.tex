\section{Reading The Display}\label{sec:display}

Spectre writes new spectra from left to right. The scan line shows where
history is being replaced. The picture stays in place; it does not scroll.
The illustration below shows a later sweep, after every column has been
filled at least once.

\begin{figure}[!htp]
\centering
\resizebox{.88\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/history.tex}}
\caption{A circular history on a fixed panel. The two shaded regions show
\emph{when} columns were written, not their signal level. Only a few columns
are drawn; Spectre retains 512 spectra.}
\label{fig:history}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Follow time around the wrap}
Read from the oldest column to the right edge, continue from the left edge,
and stop at the newest column. Columns on opposite sides of the scan line
are almost one full history apart in time.

\paragraph{First capture and pauses}
Before the first full sweep, the area ahead is empty. Paused time is excluded.
At 48 kHz a sweep takes about 10.92 seconds; see
\hyperref[sec:time]{Time And Measurement Limits} for other sample rates.
