\section{Background and Related Work}
The FFT reduces the arithmetic required to evaluate the discrete Fourier
transform~\cite{cooley1965}; real-input algorithms exploit conjugate symmetry
to avoid redundant work~\cite{sorensen1987}. Repeating a windowed transform
at a fixed hop produces a short-time spectral representation~\cite{allen1977}.
Window choice controls the familiar tradeoff between frequency resolution and
spectral leakage~\cite{harris1978}. The present work preserves these analysis
choices and changes the execution schedule.

Time distribution has direct precedents. Lo and Lee describe FFT methods for
online spectral analysis~\cite{lo1999}. Hurchalla applies a time-distributed
FFT to low-latency convolution~\cite{hurchalla2010}. Battenberg and
Avi\v{z}ienis compare cooperative and preemptive implementations of partitioned
convolution on conventional operating systems~\cite{battenberg2011}. Their
cooperative design distributes work within the host's processing thread and
uses FFTW for small transform leaves. These studies establish both the idea
of spreading FFT work and the importance of choosing an execution granularity.
The complete-analysis schedule below is a specialization for spectrum and
spectrogram displays, not an invention of time-distributed computation.

Other methods change the computation across successive frames. The sliding
DFT updates a spectrum as samples enter and leave a moving window~\cite{jacobsen2003}.
Such reuse is attractive for frequent updates, but it brings different
numerical and windowing considerations. Here each selected frame is
transformed afresh. The method supports the analyzer's existing windows and
arbitrary integer hops without maintaining a recursive spectral estimate.
Sliding transforms are an alternative design, not a measured baseline in this
study.

Finally, execution efficiency is distinct from scheduling flexibility. FFTW
selects transform decompositions for the target machine~\cite{frigo2005};
PFFFT and Apple's vDSP provide native optimized
implementations~\cite{pffft,vdsp}. We compare with all three and include
hybrids that distribute the surrounding analysis while keeping a native FFT
call intact. This exposes the practical question: how much of the benefit
requires interrupting the transform itself, and how much can be obtained by
scheduling the work around it?
