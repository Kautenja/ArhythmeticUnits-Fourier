\section{Conclusion}
This implementation study illustrates how a conventional FFT can become a
schedulable component by exposing its traversal state. The resulting operation-count budget has a
simple completion proof and preserves the order of arithmetic. Its application
also exposes two easily overlooked distinctions: completing within a requested
horizon is not the same as maintaining that frame interval, and budgeting
butterflies is not the same as budgeting a complete analysis pipeline.

The one-hop successor extends the same ordered-work argument to windowing,
packing, reconstruction, smoothing, and display preparation. Its retained-input
ring and preallocated publication slots make the data-lifetime requirements
explicit. The prototype comparison favored this design over adding three hops
of overlap latency. The integrated implementation has deterministic regression
evidence, while its device-level performance remains an open measurement task.
This distinction between scheduling bounds, numerical tests, and empirical
execution time is central to interpreting the contribution.
