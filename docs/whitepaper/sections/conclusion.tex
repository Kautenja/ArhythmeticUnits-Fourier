\section{Conclusion}
A spectrum analyzer can spread its FFT across the audio processing loop by
saving traversal state and resuming on successive sample calls. Scheduling
windowing, magnitudes, smoothing, and output in the same way brings the
complete analysis under one schedule. A balanced integer quota completes
each analysis over a chosen hop and makes the added publication delay explicit.

The measured benefit is smaller processing bursts, with costs in overhead and
result age. Matched controls establish the placement effect; native controls
and Rack measurements show when it matters and when a hybrid is preferable.
The central lesson is to evaluate total cost, peak block time, and spectrum
age together when choosing how an analyzer should share an audio engine.
