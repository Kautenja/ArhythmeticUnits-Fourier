\begin{abstract}
A spectral analyzer can be inexpensive in aggregate yet impose concentrated
work on an audio engine. This paper studies cooperative scheduling of a
complete windowed-analysis pipeline on the engine thread. The design orders
input packing, windowing, FFT butterflies, positive-bin reconstruction,
magnitude smoothing, and output preparation into bounded operations. A balanced
integer quota distributes their scheduling credits over one hop, and a complete
spectrum is published at the hop boundary. We specify the resulting work-count
bound, sample-index age, retained-input capacity, and output ownership contract.
A scalar and four-channel SIMD implementation supports live configuration
without whole-frame boundary passes in the sample path. The evaluation design
separates controlled changes in work placement from comparisons with optimized
FFT implementations, considering aggregate cost, callback-duration tails,
storage, and publication age together. Archived scalar and prototype campaigns
provide historical design evidence; final production comparisons remain to be
integrated. The contribution is an implementation and evaluation study within
established time-distributed FFT research. Bounded operations and exact
publication cadence establish a scheduling contract, while their practical
value depends on measured costs and acceptable spectrum age; they do not
establish a hard execution-time bound.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fast Fourier transform; short-time Fourier transform;
cooperative scheduling; real-time audio; spectral analysis; VCV Rack.
