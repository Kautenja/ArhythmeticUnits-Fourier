\begin{abstract}
Spectral analysis can be inexpensive on average yet concentrate enough work in
one audio callback to interfere with other processing. This report studies
cooperative execution of a complete windowed-analysis pipeline on the engine
thread. A dependency-ordered sequence covers packing, windowing, FFT
butterflies, positive-bin reconstruction, smoothing, and output preparation.
A balanced integer quota bounds scheduling credits per sample and publishes
a complete spectrum at an exact hop; we derive input-retention, sample-age,
and ownership requirements. A scalar and four-channel SIMD implementation is
evaluated against matched scheduling controls and FFTW, PFFFT, and vDSP on an
Apple M1 Pro. The confirmation campaign covers \PaperCmpWorkloads{} workloads with three
process repeats in each of three prepared same-host sessions, including
inverse jobs and complete overlap-save chains. For representative scalar
unsmoothed analysis at $N=4096$, $H=1024$, 48\,kHz, and 64-sample callbacks,
the production analyzer's simulated callback p99 is \PaperCmpCoreTail\,$\mu$s versus
\PaperCmpVdspTail\,$\mu$s for immediate vDSP, while aggregate cost rises from
\PaperCmpVdspCost\ to \PaperCmpCoreCost\,ns/sample and publication is delayed
by \PaperCmpDelayMs\,ms. Inverse and filtering controls retain bulk operations
and do not inherit the analyzer's bounded-operation contract. Results support
a conditional cost--burst--age tradeoff, not a universal speedup or an audio
deadline guarantee. The contribution is a reproducible implementation study
within established time-distributed FFT research.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fast Fourier transform; cooperative scheduling;
real-time audio; spectral analysis; inverse transform; overlap-save filtering.
