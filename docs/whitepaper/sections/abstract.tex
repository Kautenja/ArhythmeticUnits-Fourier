\begin{abstract}
Windowed spectral analysis competes with audio processing even when it produces
only display data. We present an exact-hop execution contract that distributes
window preparation, FFT butterflies, magnitude processing, smoothing, and
publication over successive engine sample calls. The contract bounds logical
work, preserves the selected input, and transfers complete spectra through an
owned mailbox. A descriptive study of 776 fresh processes in two sessions on
one Apple M1 Pro separates work placement from native FFT-kernel choice and
measures complete modules through VCV Rack's engine. For scalar
$N=4096,H=1024,D=64$ analysis, matched distribution reduces median per-hop
peak block time from \StudyBatchPeak{} to \StudyCorePeak\,$\mu$s, with
\StudyPlacementCostRatio{} times the instrumented aggregate cost.
With four aligned four-channel modules on one Rack thread, the production
pipeline, native vDSP batch, and native hybrid have typical hop peaks of
\StudyHostCorePeak, \StudyHostBatchPeak, and \StudyHostHybridPeak\,$\mu$s:
\StudyHostCoreBudgetPercent\%, \StudyHostBatchBudgetPercent\%, and
\StudyHostHybridBudgetPercent\% of the block budget. Production and full-hop
hybrid add approximately 30\,ms of logical publication age. Native hybrids
and shorter completion horizons expose useful intermediate tradeoffs, while
multithreaded host overhead and release lateness limit extrapolation from
local burst statistics to missed deadlines. The result is an analysis-specific
scheduling contract and a measured execution-granularity tradeoff, rather than
a universal performance ranking.
\end{abstract}
