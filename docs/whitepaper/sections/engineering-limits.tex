\section{Discussion and Limits}
The design establishes a publication cadence and a bound on scheduling credits,
not a performance ordering. Its usefulness depends on whether distributing work
reduces callback tails enough to justify total cost, storage, and spectrum age.
If a native batch transform fits the available budget, the additional traversal
state may offer little benefit. A worker is a viable alternative when the host
permits asynchronous analysis; overlap reuse may be useful when windows overlap
heavily or only selected bins are required. These choices need matched
measurements rather than a ranking inferred from operation counts.

The evaluation must retain unfavorable workloads and distinguish attribution
from practical comparison. A matched scheduling pair identifies the effect of
work placement within that pair. A core-versus-library comparison also changes
arithmetic, layout, and storage, so a difference cannot be assigned entirely
to suspension. A small or absent effect is informative. Callback p99, observed
maximum, aggregate cost, and sample-index age answer different questions;
none alone establishes suitability for an interactive application.

The present evidence is historical and limited to one host and compiler.
Independent direct-DFT checks cover small transforms; larger historical checks
establish scheduling equivalence. The new protocol adds independent numerical
replay and explicit comparison contracts, but implementation of that protocol
is not completed measurement evidence. Final workload selection, repetitions,
host descriptions, and comparative results remain to be integrated. Worker,
overlap-reuse, and end-to-end audio-device comparisons are not evaluated here.
Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation-agenda} retains possible extensions.

Even with a bounded quota, unequal operation costs, cache effects, preemption,
and other engine work determine elapsed callback time. A delayed call still
executes its logical quota; the schedule does not recover the missed deadline.
Likewise, producer publication does not guarantee display consumption or repaint
cadence. A slow display may intentionally skip snapshots.
