\section{Introduction}
Spectral analysis shares an audio engine's budget even when it produces no
audio output. A visualizer can have low average cost yet concentrate window
preparation, transformation, and display-data processing into one sample call.
VCV Rack makes this distinction particularly concrete: modules process one
sample at a time, and multiple engine workers synchronize between samples
\cite{rackengine}. A long call can hold up the other workers. Whether reducing
that concentration improves full-block behavior depends on the engine's own
synchronization overhead and the rest of the patch.

This paper studies complete windowed analysis on the existing engine thread.
A batch FFT minimizes state and can exploit optimized kernels. A worker thread
moves computation elsewhere, at the cost of a capture/queue/ownership policy.
Phase staggering reduces simultaneous releases when the application controls
analyzers' relative phases. Cooperative execution offers another choice: retain
an unfinished analysis and advance it during successive sample calls. It is
particularly relevant when a producer must maintain exact-hop spectral history
independently of an intermittently polling display. A UI-thread computation is
also reasonable for a latest-frame visualizer that permits dropped analyses;
that is a different delivery contract.

Making only the FFT resumable leaves frame-sized packing, reconstruction, and
output passes at its boundaries. Restarting as soon as it finishes also makes
frame cadence depend on rounding in the butterfly quota. We instead use one
dependency-ordered sequence covering the whole analyzer and a publication clock
independent of early arithmetic completion. The resulting contribution is:
\begin{itemize}
\item an explicit contract for work credits, exact-hop publication, retained
input, cancellation, and ownership of complete spectra;
\item matched placement controls and native-layout batch/hybrid alternatives
that separate scheduling from FFT-kernel choice;
\item a same-host study of native completion horizons, shipped module defaults,
and paced execution through Rack's actual multithreaded engine.
\end{itemize}

The evidence establishes a qualified result. Distributing a fixed scalar
implementation reduces its typical per-hop peak. Native hybrid pipelines can
combine smaller peaks with substantially lower total cost than scalar
butterfly scheduling. In the complete host, however, synchronization and
release lateness can dominate; a local burst reduction does not by itself
establish fewer missed releases. This makes the practical contribution a
measured way to choose execution granularity and publication age, with an
explicit distinction between analyzer and host outcomes.

The shared production implementation serves the Fourier spectrum analyzer
(four independent SIMD channels) and Spectre spectrogram (scalar) for VCV Rack.
Section~\ref{sec:one-hop} gives its contract; Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation}
and~\ref{sec:comparison-results} evaluate the new pilot evidence. The appendices
retain derivations and implementation details. Earlier timing campaigns remain
in the repository but do not support the current performance argument.
